What I work on

Research

My work sits in computational rock mechanics, at the point where continuum mechanics, numerical methods, and real rock behavior meet. I care about models that get the mechanism right — not just the curve fit.

Phase-field modeling of compaction bands

Compaction bands are thin zones of localized crushing that form in high-porosity sedimentary rocks like sandstone and limestone, and they can quietly choke off permeability in a reservoir. I develop phase-field models that capture how these bands nucleate from grain crushing, how they propagate, and how the resulting heterogeneity feeds back on fluid flow — work with direct relevance to oil and gas recovery, groundwater management, and CO₂ sequestration.

Anisotropy and moisture in clay rocks

Clay rocks and shales change shape and stiffness as they wet and dry, and that response is anisotropic in ways that simple models miss. I study how saturation-dependent anisotropy governs shrinkage and elastoplastic behavior, which matters directly for nuclear waste repositories and other engineered barriers sited in clay formations.

Multiscale homogenization for coupled processes

A rock's macroscopic hydromechanical response is set by microstructure — pore networks, mineral fabric, double-porosity effects — that's expensive to resolve directly at full scale. I build homogenization procedures that carry that microstructural detail up to models that are actually tractable to run, without losing the physics that make the material behave the way it does.
